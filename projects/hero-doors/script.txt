Two thousand years ago, an ancient Greek temple had automatic doors. In first-century Alexandria, the inventor Hero designed a wild trick. Crowds grumbled outside that those massive doors wouldn't budge. Some joked it would take twenty oxen just to crack them open. Then a priest lit a small altar fire, and the doors swung wide. Everyone gasped, convinced the gods had answered. You see, it wasn't magic. The fire heated trapped air underground, pushing water like a kettle into a hanging bucket. That heavy bucket dropped, yanking hidden ropes on the door posts. When the flame died, the doors swung shut. Turns out, the miracle was just plumbing.
