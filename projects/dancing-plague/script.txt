In July 1518, a woman named Troffea stepped into a street in Strasbourg, and started dancing. She danced all day. Then all night. Then for days. She could not stop. Within a week, dozens had joined her. Within a month, four hundred people were dancing, feet bleeding, bodies shaking, begging for a help nobody could give. The city's leaders had a brilliant idea. If people needed to dance, let them dance it out. They built a stage. They hired musicians. It made everything worse. Some dancers collapsed, and never got up. Then, as suddenly as it had begun, the dancing stopped. Five hundred years later, still nobody knows why it started.
